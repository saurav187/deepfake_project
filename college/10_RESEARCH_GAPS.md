# Research gaps the experiments can resolve

| Claim and scope | Evidence | Design decision | Observable test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Benchmark accuracy can hide domain failure | Paper 3 reports 28.55% EER from 2019 LA to ITW. Paper 15 reports 0.03% in-domain ITW and 7.10% cross-domain. The project’s v1 missed modern Gemini examples. | Publish separate seen, related-version and family-held-out trials. | Difference in EER and recall at frozen thresholds on matched clean and Opus versions, with speaker intervals. |
| Commercial-family and version coverage is incomplete | Papers 18, 32, 34 and 59 include contemporary generation, codec synthesis, OpenVoice or RVC. Published coverage of the owner’s exact voice, date, dialect and channel remains uncertain. | Record service, endpoint, model version, voice and date. Keep at least one family completely withheld. | Report exact counts and misses per family. Compare including/excluding one training family at fixed steps. |
| Postprocessing can masquerade as synthesis | Paper 32 Table 4 gives X-AASIST EERs of 33.09% on codec-generated trials and 45.33% with codec-resynthesized bona fides. Paper 42 Table 4 raises SSL-Conformer accuracy from 71.21% to 76.63% using auxiliary labels. | Human speech remains genuine after ordinary coding or enhancement. Record processing history and apply transforms to both labels. | Paired clean→Opus and actual forward FPR changes, by language and device. Include enhanced genuine controls. |
| Hindi and Indian-English phone transfer needs direct evidence | Paper 17 evaluates Bangla, 44 evaluates Arabic, 52 discusses accent variation. IndicSynth includes Hindi and its ACL paper evaluates detectors. An absolute claim that Hindi detectors do not exist is untenable. | Match Kathbath Hindi references, modern Hindi TTS and Indian-English phone speech with language-appropriate counterparts. | Separate denominators, per-language FPR, EER and abstention, including held-out speakers. [IndicSynth authors](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1070/) |
| Small models exist, but deployable transfer at a fixed risk budget remains uncertain | Paper 1 has 1.26M parameters. Paper 3 reports ~70.2 ms feature extraction and ~0.007 ms classifier time. Papers 44, 48, 58 and 59 study apps, feature cost, hosting or quantization. | Time decoding and every window on a named CPU and compare the same released weights with the teacher. | Actual bytes, warm median, cold first call, dev recall gap and false alarms. |
| Calibration and usability evidence is thinner than discrimination evidence | Paper 1 examines calibration, 14 uses likelihood-ratio cost, and 7 uses bootstrap uncertainty. V1’s release thresholds are only 0.048 apart. | Independent calibration and threshold folds plus a coverage floor. | Brier, empirical FPR/miss, coverage and class-conditional abstention on validation. |
| Evaluation protocol changes the meaning of a score | Paper 7 retrains on additional corpora. Paper 57 splits ITW internally. Paper 36 explicitly uses breathing and silence. In v1 only four IndieFake dev speakers constrain inference. | Hold out lineage, test metadata-only predictors, keep content variants together. | Cross-fold ID/hash collisions, directional AUC of nuisance probes and speaker-clustered intervals. |
| Partial edits and short clips need separate targets | Paper 4 localizes splice boundaries. V1 caught 59.5% of historical short ITW fakes. | Abstain on insufficient or very short spans and reserve a dedicated edited set. | Coverage by usable length, event recall and timing error only where segment ground truth exists. |

VoxENES and ITW are release confirmations. Earlier published results above are background evidence. Development choices use the separate source, speaker and generator folds.
