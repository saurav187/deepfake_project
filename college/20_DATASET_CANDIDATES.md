# Which data to acquire after the English shortcut audit

**First acquisition: LibriSeVoc v1, conditional on clearing LibriSpeech parent/reader overlap.** It contains the genuine recording and six reconstructions from that recording's own mel spectrogram. This is a stronger timing control than synthesising new speech from the same text. It also gives us known target readers without another voice-labelling run. Its 45.05 GB ZIP is a substantial transfer. If the shared LibriSpeech ancestry cannot be cleared from public provenance or an existing approved exclusion list, start with the **0.487 GB CodecFake schema sample** instead. Neither corpus has a published pass on our exact four-second audit. The normalised English audit still measured upper-band AUC 0.817 and speech-ratio AUC 0.659. Which generators drove those differences remains unverified here. [Run handoff](13_HANDOFF_TO_CLAUDE.md) · [LibriSeVoc paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023W/WMF/papers/Sun_AI-Synthesized_Voice_Detection_Using_Neural_Vocoder_Artifacts_CVPRW_2023_paper.pdf) · [CodecFake paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07237v1)

## Evidence by candidate

**1. LibriSeVoc v1 (Zenodo 15127251).** English, 13,201 genuine WAVs from LibriTTS and 79,206 reconstructions, six per recording, at 24 kHz and 5–34 seconds. The six systems are WaveNet, WaveRNN, MelGAN, Parallel WaveGAN, WaveGrad and DiffWave. Known parent, words, reader and source timing make reader-disjoint experiments possible. The unique reader count after exclusions is unverified. Genuine speech supplies the spectrogram but does not pass through the fake vocoder. A common sample rate and inherited pauses make rate, duration, edges and speech ratio less promising shortcuts. Different vocoders can change RMS, upper-band energy, rolloff and tilt. The authors explicitly study these vocoder artifacts. No parity of effective bandwidth or common final gain processing has been established. Reserve a complete vocoder family for transfer testing if the remaining dataset is viable. Compare reader, chapter and sentence across PhonemeDF, LibriSpeech dev and existing folds before assignment. VoxENES uses English LibriSpeech material, so different filenames or a different segmentation are insufficient to clear it. CC BY-SA allows attributed commercial reuse and redistribution under share-alike conditions for adaptations. Plan key `librisevoc`, ZIP MD5 `bee5261df62de16ef900861358afae08`. [Record and terms](https://zenodo.org/records/15127251) · [Paper and generation table](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023W/WMF/papers/Sun_AI-Synthesized_Voice_Detection_Using_Neural_Vocoder_Artifacts_CVPRW_2023_paper.pdf) · [Public VoxENES source description](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11706v1)

**2. CodecFake (Interspeech 2024, arXiv 2406.07237).** Use the specific HF repository linked by the authors, `rogertseng/CodecFake`, revision `ae224c9cff0e7a19edf885a670c2c4d1742d3e1e7`. The paper used 107 English VCTK speakers, 15 models from six frameworks (SpeechTokenizer, AcademiCodec, AudioDec, DAC, EnCodec and FunCodec). They reconstruct an individual real waveform. This preserves identity, words and much of the timing. The paper reserves just two speakers each for validation and testing, below our five-reader minimum. A new assignment from eligible development material needs demonstrated counts. The HF package exposes about 708k rows in 161 train shards (44,242 VCTK parents in the paper across 103/2/2 readers); the viewer shows durations including 0.6–19.3 s in its preview. Codec models use different rates, including 16, 24 and 44.1 kHz. The common stored waveform encoding and complete duration range are unverified. Real controls bypass neural coding. Timing/edge risk should be lower than independent TTS, while cutoffs, spectral tilt, reconstruction gain and short-clip eligibility could predict labels. The paper reports transfer experiments, not this nuisance audit. The visible schema shows speaker, codec and label; verify that embedded paths or a publisher map recover each original VCTK utterance and microphone. One shard may contain just spoofs. If original controls are absent, join to the exact VCTK release; a speaker alone cannot establish the pair. Reserve all ten PhonemeDF VCTK reference identities across folds and crosswalk ASVspoof before combining. Commercial use and redistribution with attribution under CC BY 4.0. Plan key `codecfake-sample`, LFS SHA-256 pinned in code. [Publisher](https://codecfake.github.io/) · [Methods and splits](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07237v1) · [Release, schema and licence](https://huggingface.co/datasets/rogertseng/CodecFake/tree/ae224c9cff0e7a19edf885a670c2c4d1742d3e1e7)

**3. ASVspoof 2019 LA (DOI 10.7488/ds/2555).** One LA ZIP, English VCTK, 107 source speakers, 20 target speakers each in official train and development. The LA partition contains 25,380 train, 24,844 development and 71,237 evaluation utterances. The corpus uses 16 kHz FLAC. Full clip-duration range unverified. Train/dev include four synthesis systems and two VC systems (A01–A06), with concatenative, parametric, WaveNet and conversion approaches. Evaluation adds the A07–A19 attack conditions. Preserve official evaluation as reserved. Source corpus and target identity are documented; utterance pairing and microphone equivalence must be established with the official VCTK maps. Real speech bypasses synthesis. Shared rate/container help only two probes. A published analysis found that silence alone separated labels; speech ratio, edges and crop attrition remain serious concerns, as do RMS and spectral fields. Prefer as a diagnostic before mixing with CodecFake or the project's VCTK targets. ODC-By permits attributed database reuse, including commercial use; retain the notices and consult the release licence for individual contents. Plan key `asvspoof19-la`, MD5 `30c98f11d8b2bc21f2c257bfd78bb5c5`. [Data, licence and checksums](https://zenodo.org/records/6906306) · [Protocol](https://www.asvspoof.org/asvspoof2019/asvspoof2019_evaluation_plan.pdf) · [Publisher source maps](https://www.asvspoof.org/index2019.html) · [Measured silence bias](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.12914)

**4. Indic-CodecFake (ACL Findings 2026, paper 2159).** The authors reconstructed IndicSUPERB utterances in 12 languages including Hindi with DAC, EnCodec, SNAC, SoundStream, SpeechTokenizer, FunCodec, AudioDec and Mimi. Across variants, native rates span 16–48 kHz. This offers the closest Hindi parent/reader/timing match; the genuine waveform bypasses the decoder. Existing Kathbath/IndicSUPERB parent and reader IDs must inherit their existing folds, including crossings with IndicSynth reference/target speakers. Expect lower pacing and edge risk, but codec-specific bandwidth, gain and high-frequency suppression can still fail. The paper reports cross-language detector degradation. Total release size, audio file count, delivered codec/rate, duration range, Hindi reader counts, usable parent keys, public archive, checksums, data licence, redistribution, price and viable download command are **unverified**. The project hosts demos and links codec implementations. The paper licence does not establish reusable audio rights. Ask the authors for a Hindi manifest, data terms and access before considering transfer. Count at least five eligible readers in *each* evaluation fold after the existing four-second and parent checks. [Project and generation procedure](https://helixometry.github.io/IndicFake/) · [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.2159/)

**5. IndicTTS Deepfake Challenge (HF revision 57347517658ae989597d8cef303cffb647ed2434).** The publisher reports 31,102 labelled train clips across 16 languages including Hindi and English, plus 2,664 test clips with hidden labels. The release contains 35 training and four test Parquet files. The card exposes ID, language, text and TTS label; preview includes clips much shorter than four seconds. Archive-wide rate, embedded codec, complete duration range, real recording corpus, distinct readers, number/names of TTS systems, real/fake speaker and recording correspondence, equal postprocessing, Hindi count after cropping, published bias study and sealed-source independence are unverified. This is a low-cost metadata inspection with potentially high spectral, level, timing and container risk. Ask for ID semantics before trusting an apparent speaker code. Only admit Hindi if reader-disjoint splits and paired lineage survive the minimums. Card licence CC BY 4.0 permits attributed redistribution and commercial reuse. Plan key `indictts-sample`, train shard only, LFS SHA-256 in code. Its language composition is unverified. [Card and schema](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SherryT997/IndicTTS-Deepfake-Challenge-Data) · [Pinned files](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SherryT997/IndicTTS-Deepfake-Challenge-Data/tree/57347517658ae989597d8cef303cffb647ed2434/data)

**6. SEA-Spoof (DOI 10.57967/hf/9195).** The cleaned HF release has 38 Parquet shards and 553,746 clips including 71,171 Hindi, with English, Tamil, Thai, Malay, Indonesian and Vietnamese also represented. Both labels use 16 kHz FLAC. The authors describe 14 systems across the original study: VITS-MMS, Edge-TTS, XTTS-v2, FastSpeech2, Indic-TTS, F5-TTS, Tacotron2, MelGAN-FastSpeech2, FastPitch, Glow-TTS, HeyGen, ElevenLabs, MiniMax and OpenAI TTS. Hindi coverage by individual system is unverified. Hindi genuines come from Common Voice and Indic speech corpora. Transcript provenance includes backfills; check the exact-text flag. Speaker identity, same-recording pairs, same effective bandwidth, duration range and five retained readers per Hindi evaluation fold are unverified. The paper reports cross-language degradation. Rate and file codec are controlled; source/channel diversity, gain, pacing and spectrum remain high-risk. Existing XTTS and ElevenLabs family coverage overlaps; separate any shared readers or sources, especially public-media material. Use only as an academic modern-family diagnostic pending provenance. Manual author/HF approval, no commercial use or redistribution to unapproved users, no public fee listed. After approval only, `hf download Jack-ppkdczgx/SEA-Spoof --repo-type dataset --revision 132f5dca9b6efe39cf1d3b54a858f167f5a421fc --include 'data/train/*' --local-dir "$D/development/sea-spoof"`. SHA-256 per shard in HF LFS metadata. [Access, fields and counts](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Jack-ppkdczgx/SEA-Spoof) · [Generation and source corpora](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19865v1)

**7. CSLU 22 Languages v1.2 (LDC2005S26, DOI 10.35111/zkn2-5x88).** Genuine telephone speech in 21 released languages including Hindi and English. 50,191 utterances, approximately 84 hours. No fake systems. Digital telephone collection at 8 kHz, 8-bit µ-law, distributed in 16-bit linear RIFF. Fixed vocabulary and spontaneous speech; distinct callers, per-clip durations, authorised download size/checksum and current account fee are unverified. Real phone-channel high-frequency loss and telephony gain are precisely why this is a false-alarm panel. Use genuine-only evaluation at an already frozen threshold; a real/fake audit AUC cannot be calculated on this set alone. There is no documented overlap with the owned audiobook/studio corpora. Identity overlap with private sets remains unverified. Account-based web download after owner accepts the CSLU agreement; no public pinned download command. The agreement restricts use to non-commercial linguistic education/research and prohibits redistribution outside the named group. A commercial licence is separate. [CSLU agreement](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/license/cslu-corpora-non-commercial-research-only.pdf) [LDC collection, licence and access](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2005S26)

## Contamination review and acceptance decision

I compared **published parent corpora, collection descriptions and speaker namespaces** against the project's documented sealed sources and Drive holdings. No sealed manifest, recording, speaker lookup or audio fingerprint was opened. That comparison identifies risks, not proof of zero speaker overlap. VoxENES shares LibriSpeech ancestry; ITW and Deepfake-Eval contain public-media material; VA, app testing and future owner recordings have no public identity manifest for this check. Source ambiguity in a mixed web corpus remains unresolved. For all seven candidates, exact clip/person overlap with private or undisclosed sources is **unverified**. Use only development-side provenance or an existing permitted exclusion list. If parent or reader independence cannot be established, withhold those records from decision folds. [Project dataset provenance](11_DATASETS_V3.md) · [VoxENES methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11706v1)

For any approved intake, first count eligible readers, target voices, parents and families after the exact four-second transform, per language and fold. Keep all reconstructions of each source together, reserve entire synthesis families, and compare each fake family against its own genuine parents. Preserve the current thresholds, reader/voice/text separation and existing sealed evaluations. A high nuisance AUC can be a real codec fingerprint while still failing this project's gate. No candidate has demonstrated that every individual and joint probe passes 0.60 on this pipeline.

## Rejected or deferred

- **WaveFake.** English reconstructions share a genuine LJSpeech parent, but LJSpeech has one reader. The Japanese data do not expand English reader coverage. The fake ZIP alone is 28.92 GB. Useful for a single-voice vocoder diagnostic, unsuitable for the current reader-disjoint pilot. [Publisher release](https://zenodo.org/records/5642694) · [LJSpeech](https://keithito.com/LJ-Speech-Dataset/)
- **ASVspoof 2021 LA/DF and ASVspoof 5.** 2021 offers telephony/compression conditions on both labels, but its challenge material is evaluation data with documented 2019/VCTK/VCC lineage. Free Zenodo downloads, ODC-By; preserve evaluation separation. ASVspoof 5 adds crowdsourced bona fide speech and modern TTS/VC plus adversarial conditions; much broader recording chains, with no equally processed per-parent pairs established here. Direct public multipart release; consult its LICENSE for reuse. Defer as a first repair. [2021 access, keys and crosswalk](https://www.asvspoof.org/index2021.html) · [ASVspoof 5 release](https://zenodo.org/records/14498691) · [Authors' design paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0885230825000506)
- **More MLAAD, PhonemeDF or the same Hindi pairing.** Existing M-AILABS parent matches already support a descriptive English panel. Picking more fake systems does not by itself match effective bandwidth or add Hindi source readers. The existing Hindi data remains useful only if a genuinely different parent/reader assignment can meet both independence and the minima. [Local evidence](17_STAGE0_REPORT.md) · [IndicSynth card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/vdivyasharma/IndicSynth)
- **RTCFake.** A reasonable future shared-channel panel, but the current challenge HF release requires official team registration. Source ancestry needs review for reused benchmark speech before considering it. [Publisher access terms](https://huggingface.co/datasets/JunXueTech/RTCFake)

## Offline transfer plan

From the repository root, the following **prints** exact byte counts, versioned URLs, published checksums and individual resumable shell commands. It needs no network and creates no files. The two HF samples are deliberately bounded intake checks. Plan each source independently; approve before transferring. The printed commands retain interrupted partials and verify size and hash before promotion. If an existing file or complete partial fails verification, inspect it before retrying.

```bash
venv/bin/python -m pipeline_v3.fetch_candidates --data /content/drive/MyDrive/audio-v3 \
  --sources librisevoc codecfake-sample asvspoof19-la indictts-sample --dry-run
```
